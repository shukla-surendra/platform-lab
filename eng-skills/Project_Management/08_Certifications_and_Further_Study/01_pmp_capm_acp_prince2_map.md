# Certifications and Bodies of Knowledge — A Map, Not a Recommendation to Pursue One

This folder's stated purpose is literacy, not certification (`../README.md`). This chapter exists anyway, briefly, because the certification landscape is exactly the kind of thing that gets referenced constantly in PM conversations ("she's PMP-certified," "we follow PRINCE2 here") without ever being explained — and because it closes out the "everything from PMI" scope precisely by naming its edges.

## 1. PMI's Certification Ladder

| Certification | Full name | Who it's for | Rough prerequisite |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CAPM** | Certified Associate in Project Management | Entry-level, or those wanting a foundational credential without extensive experience | 23 hours of PM education; no work-experience requirement |
| **PMP** | Project Management Professional | Experienced project managers; the industry's most recognized general PM credential | ~36–60 months leading projects (varies by education level) + 35 hours of PM education |
| **PMI-ACP** | PMI Agile Certified Practitioner | Practitioners working primarily in agile environments | Agile project experience + agile-specific training hours |
| **PgMP** | Program Management Professional | Program managers overseeing multiple related projects | Extensive program-level experience |
| **PfMP** | Portfolio Management Professional | Portfolio managers making strategic, cross-project resourcing decisions | Extensive portfolio-level experience |
| **PMI-RMP** | Risk Management Professional | Specialists focused specifically on project risk | Risk-management-specific experience + training |
| **PMI-SP** | Scheduling Professional | Specialists focused specifically on project scheduling | Scheduling-specific experience + training |

The exam most relevant to the vocabulary in this folder is the **PMP**, since it tests the full PMBOK breadth this folder samples from; the **PMI-ACP** is the one most relevant to an engineer working primarily in Scrum/Kanban-run teams, since it tests `../03_Methodologies/01_predictive_agile_and_hybrid_delivery.md`'s content in much greater depth.

## 2. PRINCE2 — the Non-PMI Alternative

**PRINCE2** (**PR**ojects **IN** **C**ontrolled **E**nvironments) is a UK-originated, process-driven PM methodology — more common in Europe, government, and UK-headquartered enterprises than PMBOK-flavored PM, which dominates in North America. Where PMBOK is a *body of knowledge* (a reference framework a PM draws from and tailors), PRINCE2 is a *prescriptive method* (a defined sequence of processes to follow).

| PRINCE2 concept | Rough PMBOK equivalent |
|---|---|
| **Project Board** | Steering committee / sponsor group |
| **Business Case** | Project charter's business-need section, kept as a living document throughout |
| **Stages** | Phases, with a mandatory formal review (End Stage Assessment) between each |
| **Tolerances** | Pre-authorized limits (time, cost, scope, risk, quality, benefit) within which a manager can proceed without escalating — a formalized version of PMBOK's less-explicit "threshold" concept |
| **Exception Report** | A formal report raised the moment a tolerance is breached — the PRINCE2 analogue of an amber-to-red status escalation |

If working with a UK or European client/team, hearing "stage boundary" or "tolerance" instead of "phase gate" or "threshold" is very likely PRINCE2 vocabulary, not a different concept — the conversion table in `../04_Glossary_Formulas_Conversions/02_formulas_and_conversions.md` §6 extends naturally to this mapping as well.

## 3. PMBOK vs. PRINCE2 vs. Agile Practice Guide, at a Glance

| | PMBOK Guide | PRINCE2 | Agile Practice Guide (PMI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | Descriptive body of knowledge, tailored per project | Prescriptive method, followed as defined | Descriptive guide to agile approaches, published jointly by PMI and the Agile Alliance |
| **Origin** | PMI (USA-headquartered, global reach) | UK government (originally), now Axelos-owned | PMI, as a companion volume to PMBOK |
| **Best fit** | Broad applicability across industries and project types | Structured, governance-heavy environments (government, large regulated programs) | Product development with evolving requirements |
| **Certification path** | CAPM → PMP → PgMP/PfMP | PRINCE2 Foundation → PRINCE2 Practitioner | PMI-ACP |

## 4. Is Certification Worth Pursuing, Given the Stated Goal

Given this folder's explicit premise — literacy to communicate and collaborate effectively as an engineer/architect, not a career pivot into project management — a certification is **not required** to get the value documented across this entire folder; the vocabulary, mental models, and templates here are the actual payload, and they transfer into daily communication whether or not a credential is ever earned.

That said, two situations where pursuing one anyway has genuine, non-vocabulary value:

- **PMI-ACP**, if operating for a sustained period as something close to a Scrum Master or delivery lead in addition to the engineering role — the credential signals to an employer or client that agile delivery mechanics are a demonstrated, not just conversational, skill.
- **CAPM**, as a structured forcing function for going deeper than this folder if self-study without external accountability doesn't stick — some learners genuinely need an exam date to complete the reading, the same "fix the exposure date before the prep plan" principle from `../../Communication-Mastery/02_Thinking_Frameworks/06_prerequisite_stacking_and_the_elsewhere_effect.md`.

Neither is a prerequisite for anything in the rest of this folder, and both are explicitly optional extensions, not the goal.

## 6. Glossary — Vocabulary Used in This Chapter

| Term/Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Prescriptive (method) | Defining a specific, required sequence of steps to follow |
| Descriptive (body of knowledge) | Providing a reference framework to be tailored, rather than followed exactly |
| Tolerance (PRINCE2) | A pre-authorized limit within which a manager may proceed without escalating |
| Exception Report | A formal report raised when a tolerance is breached |
| Forcing function | A mechanism that compels an action or decision that might otherwise be endlessly deferred |
| Payload | (figurative) The actual valuable content, as distinct from its packaging or delivery mechanism |
