# 12-Week Progressive Assignments

A complementary structure to `02_challenges_30_60_90.md` — where that document is a daily-habit program, this is a set of discrete, concrete weekly deliverables. Use whichever structure fits how you work, or run them together (they're designed to be compatible — the daily routine feeds these weekly deliverables).

Each assignment produces a **real artifact** — not just practice reps, but something you could actually show someone or reuse later.

---

### Week 1 — Baseline and Diagnosis
**Assignment:** Record 5 explanations of real systems you work on, no framework applied, just talk naturally. Transcribe or re-watch all 5. Using `13_Common_Mistakes`, write a one-page diagnosis: your top 3 recurring patterns, named specifically (not "I ramble" but "I state my conclusion as the last sentence in 4 of 5 recordings").
**Artifact:** A written self-diagnosis doc.

### Week 2 — PREP Fluency
**Assignment:** Write out 10 PREP-structured answers (`03_Explanation_Frameworks/01`) to real "why did you..." questions about your work. Record yourself delivering all 10 from memory (not reading), under 45 seconds each.
**Artifact:** 10 recorded PREP answers + written versions for reference.

### Week 3 — STAR Story Bank, Part 1
**Assignment:** Build 4 complete STAR stories (`08_Interview_Communication/01`) from your real experience, covering at minimum: an incident, a hard technical decision, a disagreement, and a failure. Record each at 60-90 seconds.
**Artifact:** 4 written + recorded STAR stories — the start of your permanent story bank.

### Week 4 — Phrase Library Fluency Check
**Assignment:** Without looking at `05_Phrase_Library`, write from memory as many phrases as you can recall for: openers, trade-off language, disagreement language, incident language. Compare against the actual library. Drill the gaps daily for the rest of the week.
**Artifact:** A gap list + a week of targeted drilling logged in your training log.

### Week 5 — Architecture Walkthrough
**Assignment:** Pick the most complex real system you own. Write a full Architecture Walkthrough (`07_Architecture_Communication/01`) — context, requirements, shape, 3 key decisions, failure modes, out-of-scope. Deliver it live to a real colleague if possible; record it either way, 5+ minutes.
**Artifact:** A written walkthrough doc + a recording.

### Week 6 — Incident Narrative / Postmortem
**Assignment:** Take a real past incident (or a plausible one from `11_Exercises/2.3` if you don't have a recent real one) and write a full postmortem using the template in `09_Meeting_Communication/01`, plus deliver the spoken 90-second version (`04_Technical_Storytelling/02`).
**Artifact:** A written postmortem + a recorded spoken summary.

### Week 7 — Executive Summary Practice
**Assignment:** Take 3 real technical decisions or projects and write executive summaries for each (`06_Project_Presentation/01`), under 100 words each, outcome-first, zero unexplained jargon. Have a non-technical person (if available) read one and tell you what they understood — use their feedback directly.
**Artifact:** 3 written executive summaries + feedback notes.

### Week 8 — Disagreement and Feedback Drills
**Assignment:** Identify 3 real technical opinions you hold that someone has disagreed with. Practice all 3 disagreement patterns from `05_Phrase_Library/03`, live with a practice partner if possible (cold, no advance script — see `11_Exercises/3.7`). Also draft written feedback on a real piece of work (code review, design doc) using the labeled-severity pattern from `07_Architecture_Communication/01`.
**Artifact:** Notes from 3 disagreement reps + one real piece of labeled feedback delivered.

### Week 9 — System Design Mock
**Assignment:** Run one full 30-45 minute system design mock (`11_Exercises/3.1`), solo or with a partner, fully recorded, narrating every step of the framework (`08_Interview_Communication/01`).
**Artifact:** A full recording + a self-review using `12_Recording_Analysis/02`.

### Week 10 — Behavioral Interview Mock
**Assignment:** Using your now-larger story bank, answer 8 behavioral questions back to back (`08_Interview_Communication/02` for prompts), 90 seconds each, with a "what would you do differently" follow-up on each, recorded.
**Artifact:** A full recorded mock interview session.

### Week 11 — Full Case Study Build
**Assignment:** Using the Reusable Case Study Template (`14_Advanced/01`), build one complete case study from your best real project — problem through executive summary through likely interview questions.
**Artifact:** One complete, polished case study document — a genuinely reusable asset for interviews and promotion packets.

### Week 12 — Full Compression + Retrospective
**Assignment:** Take your Week 11 case study and deliver it at all 4 lengths from the Compression Ladder (`11_Exercises/3.6`): 10 sec, 30 sec, 90 sec, 5 min, all recorded. Then write a retrospective: compare your Week 1 baseline recordings to your Week 12 recordings directly, and set your focus for the next cycle.
**Artifact:** 4 compressed recordings of the same content + a written 12-week retrospective.

## Assignment Submission Format (If Working With a Mentor or Peer Reviewer)

If you have someone reviewing your work through this program (a mentor, a peer, a coach), use this consistent format each week so feedback stays comparable across weeks:

```markdown
## Week [N] Submission

Assignment: ____________
Artifact(s): [link/attach]
Self-assessed score (1-3) on: Structure / Simplification / Delivery
What I found hardest this week: ____________
Specific feedback requested: ____________
```

## If You're Doing This Solo

Solo is entirely viable — the recording + self-review loop (`12_Recording_Analysis`) is designed to work without a second person. But if you can recruit even one colleague for occasional live feedback (Weeks 8, 9, 10 benefit most from a real second person), the calibration you get on how you actually land with another human is something self-review alone can't fully replace — self-scoring tends to drift over time without an external check.

## Glossary — Vocabulary Used in This Chapter

| Term / Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Artifact** | A tangible, produced output of work — something concrete that can be shown or reused, not just an exercise. |
| **Deliverable** | A concrete piece of work committed to and produced by a deadline. |
| **Calibration** | Adjusting one's judgment or self-assessment to match an accurate, external standard. |
| **Land** (informal) | To be received or have the intended effect on an audience. |
| **Drift** | To gradually shift away from an accurate baseline over time, without a corrective check. |
| **Cold** (unprepared) | Attempted without advance warning or a rehearsed script. |
| **Recruit** (a person) | To persuade someone to take part in or help with an activity. |
| **Viable** | Capable of working successfully on its own. |
