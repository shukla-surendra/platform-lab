# Self-Review Checklist

The full checklist for weekly deep-review passes (`01_how_to_record_and_review.md`). Organized by the same five-layer structure from `01_Foundations/03`, plus delivery mechanics.

## Structure Checklist

- [ ] **Was my structure clear?** Could someone else name the framework I used (PREP/STAR/SCQA) just from listening?
- [ ] **Did I answer first?** Is the core claim in the first sentence, or does it show up buried in the middle or at the end?
- [ ] **Did I use one clear reason/mechanism**, or did I list multiple competing reasons that diluted the main point?
- [ ] **Did I include a concrete example** (a number, a specific case, a real story) — or was everything abstract?
- [ ] **Did I name a trade-off or limitation**, or did the explanation sound like a sales pitch with no honest cost?
- [ ] **Did I end with an explicit so-what** — or did I just trail off, leaving the listener to infer the point?

## Simplification Checklist

- [ ] **Did I simplify enough for the audience I was targeting?** Would a junior engineer in that audience have followed it?
- [ ] **Did I use jargon without defining it**, assuming shared context that might not exist? (Curse of knowledge — `01_Foundations/02`)
- [ ] **Did I use an analogy, and if so, does it hold up** if I try to find where it breaks? (`01_Foundations/02`, `11_Exercises/1.3`)
- [ ] **Could I have said this in half the words** without losing meaning? (Completeness bias check — `01_Foundations/01`)

## Delivery Checklist

- [ ] **Did I pause, or fill every gap with "um"/"like"/"so basically"?** Count filler words this pass.
- [ ] **Was my pacing steady, or did I speed up when uncertain** (a common tell of low confidence in a specific section)?
- [ ] **Did my volume/energy stay consistent**, or did it drop noticeably at the point where I lost structure?
- [ ] **Did I finish within my time target?** If not, which section ran long — usually it's Situation/setup (see `08_Interview_Communication/01`).

## Storytelling Checklist (If Applicable)

- [ ] **Did I include a genuine moment of tension or uncertainty**, or did I skip straight from problem to resolved? (`04_Technical_Storytelling/01`)
- [ ] **Did I include at least one specific number and one specific time reference?**
- [ ] **Was my "stable state" opening brief**, or did I over-invest in setup before the actual disruption/point?

## Altitude Checklist

- [ ] **Did I pick the right altitude for this audience** on purpose, or did I default to my own comfort level (usually too technical)? (`01_Foundations/03`)
- [ ] **Could I have shifted altitude cleanly if interrupted with a follow-up**, or would I have had to restart?

## The "Could a Junior Engineer Understand?" Test

This deserves its own line because it's the single fastest diagnostic for over-complexity. Play back your recording and genuinely ask: would a new-grad engineer on an adjacent team follow this without needing three clarifying questions? If not, identify the specific sentence where they'd get lost — usually it's an undefined acronym, an unexplained assumption, or a jump in the logical chain that felt obvious to you but wasn't stated.

## Scoring Template (Weekly Deep Review)

Use a simple 1-3 scale per category rather than trying to be precise — the point is trend visibility over time, not a perfect score.

```markdown
## Weekly Review — [date]

Recording topic: ____________
Framework used: ____________

Structure (1-3):       ___   Notes: ____________
Simplification (1-3):  ___   Notes: ____________
Delivery (1-3):        ___   Notes: ____________
Storytelling (1-3):    ___   Notes: ____________ (N/A if not applicable)
Altitude (1-3):        ___   Notes: ____________

Filler word count:     ___
Time target vs actual: ___ / ___

ONE thing to focus on next week: ____________
```

## How to Use a Low Score Productively

A low score in any category is diagnostic, not discouraging — it points directly at which chapter of this repository to revisit:

| Low score in... | Revisit |
|---|---|
| Structure | `02_Thinking_Frameworks`, `03_Explanation_Frameworks` |
| Simplification | `03_Explanation_Frameworks/02` (Feynman Technique), `11_Exercises` Tier 1 |
| Delivery | `10_Daily_Practice`, pause/pacing drills |
| Storytelling | `04_Technical_Storytelling` |
| Altitude | `01_Foundations/03`, `11_Exercises/1.1` (five audiences) |

## The Most Important Rule of Self-Review

**Fix one thing per review cycle, not five.** Trying to simultaneously improve structure, filler words, pacing, and storytelling in the same week produces diffuse, hard-to-notice progress on all four instead of clear progress on one. Pick the single lowest-scoring category, focus the next week's practice specifically on it, and let the others hold steady. This mirrors the "one thing to fix tomorrow" rule in the daily quick-pass (`01`) — specificity and restraint compound; trying to fix everything at once usually fixes nothing.

## Glossary — Vocabulary Used in This Chapter

| Term / Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Curse of knowledge** | The cognitive bias where knowing something well makes it hard to imagine not knowing it, leading to unexplained jargon. |
| **Trail off** | To gradually fade out or stop speaking without a clean, deliberate ending. |
| **Dilute** | To weaken the strength or impact of something (here, a main point) by adding too much surrounding material. |
| **A tell** | A subtle, involuntary signal that reveals an underlying state (borrowed from poker — here, a tell of low confidence). |
| **Diagnostic** | Serving to identify the specific nature or cause of a problem, rather than just its symptoms. |
| **From scratch** | Starting over completely, from the very beginning. |
| **Diffuse** | Spread out thinly over a wide area, rather than concentrated — here, progress that's hard to notice because it's spread across too many goals at once. |
| **Restraint** | Deliberate self-control — here, resisting the urge to fix everything at once. |
| **Compound** (verb) | To build up or intensify over time through repeated, cumulative effect. |
